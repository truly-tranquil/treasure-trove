Session 4 — Goals & Self-Regulation (≈65 (100) min)¶

Focus: SMART goals; process vs. outcome goals; Zimmerman's self-regulation cycle; the weekly reflection prompt. CLOs: 3 (primary), 1 (applied).

Deposit: SMART goals sheet (3+ goals) with Zimmerman placement, and a Zimmerman cycle sheet for one goal.

Warm-up from memory (5 min)¶

Name the three needs in Self-Determination Theory and say which one is weakest for you right now (check your Handout 1-C rating). Then check your notes.

Learn (15 (45) min)¶

  • Read (10 min): Purdue Global Academic Success Center, "Teaching Students to Learn." A short, free overview of the three phases of self-regulation, with quotes from Zimmerman. It is written for faculty, so read it for the phases and examples.
  • Core idea (5 min): SMART goals, and process vs. outcome. A SMART goal is:
  • Specific (what exactly?)
  • Measurable (how will you know?)
  • Achievable (challenging but doable given your load)
  • Relevant (tied to your "why")
  • Time-bound (it has a deadline) Goals also come in two kinds. An outcome goal is the result you want ("pass the certification exam"). A process goal is the behavior you control ("study three evenings a week"). Outcomes depend partly on things you can't control, so pair each outcome goal with a process goal. Handout 1-D has a row for checking which kind you wrote.
  • Optional read (30 min): Zimmerman (2002), "Becoming a Self-Regulated Learner: An Overview" (Theory Into Practice).

Do (45 (50) min)¶

  1. Build your SMART goals sheet (20 min). Choose three goals for the next 4–12 weeks. They don't all have to be academic: one can be well-being, one social, one a skill. Fill in every row for the goal you most want to keep (Goal 1). For Goals 2 and 3, fill in at least the metric, the deadline, and a first action step you can do within 48 hours; you can come back for the other rows later. Use Handout 1-D.
  2. Place each goal in the cycle (5 min). For each goal, mark forethought, performance, or self-reflection. For Goal 1, write what the next loop looks like. (Most goals set this week are in forethought. That's fine.)
  3. Complete a Zimmerman cycle sheet for Goal 1 (10 min). Fill in Handout 1-E. In the strategy box, a placeholder is fine (for example, "a timed 25-minute block"). Week 2 gives you real study strategies, and you'll swap one in then.
  4. Check your obstacle for a hidden belief (5 min). Look at the obstacle you named on Handout 1-E. If it hides a fixed statement ("I'm just not a morning person," "I always lose motivation"), run it through a Handout 1-B card: add "yet," name a next step, connect a resource. If it's purely practical (no quiet space, not enough time), skip the card and use the five minutes to sharpen your if–then.
  5. Set up your rhythms (5 min). Put the weekly reflection prompt where you'll see it, and set a reminder for the 5-minute bedtime reflection starting tonight. Partner version (5 min, optional): trade sheets and ask one question per goal: "How will you measure that?" or "What might get in the way?" Share only what you're comfortable sharing.
