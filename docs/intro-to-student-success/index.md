# STU-101 · Student Success 101
 
*A 4-week, self-paced curriculum*
 
## Course at a glance
 
| | |
|---|---|
| **Course code** | STU-101 |
| **Duration** | 4 weeks · about 5 hours per week (≈20 hours total) · 16 core sessions, with the capstone built across the course |
| **Format** | Self-guided. Every live or partner activity has a solo version, and every module has low-tech and no-tech options. |
| **Prerequisites** | None |
| **Support** | There is no instructor feedback loop, so each week has built-in self-checks (a confidence check at the start, a retrieval check at the end). You may also contact Killian with any questions or concerns. |
| **Free spine text** | OpenStax *College Success* (free web view and PDF): <https://openstax.org/details/books/college-success> |
 
**Pacing.** Each week is a 10-minute start-of-week confidence check, four sessions of about 65 minutes, and a 15-minute end-of-week self-check, which comes to roughly 5 hours. Every session lists its read, watch, and listen items with a time estimate and a link to a free, official or publisher-hosted source.
 
## Course learning objectives (CLOs)
 
By the end of STU-101, students will be able to:
 
1. **Model self:** Describe their own learning profile, strengths, and challenges, and articulate a personal motivation statement.
2. **Adopt a growth mindset:** Apply growth-mindset thinking to setbacks and reframe fixed "I can't" statements.
3. **Set and govern goals:** Set SMART goals and run a self-regulation cycle (forethought → performance → self-reflection).
4. **Learn how to learn:** Apply evidence-based study strategies (retrieval practice, spaced practice, interleaving, effective note-taking).
5. **Plan time and work:** Build a realistic weekly plan, break down large tasks (including when procrastination gets in the way), and manage a multi-course workload.
6. **Read and process academic text:** Use structured reading (survey–question–read–recite–review) and annotate actively.
7. **Prepare for assessment:** Use exam and assignment preparation routines that target understanding, not last-minute cramming.
8. **Sustain performance:** Connect well-being, campus and community resources, and persistence to long-term success, and draft a personal success plan.

## Module 1 — Foundations & Mindset: "Who Am I as a Learner?"
 
**Theme:** Before technique, identity. You can't regulate learning you haven't examined.
 
**CLOs in play:** 1, 2, 3
 
### Learning objectives
 
1. Identify their own learning profile: strengths, challenges, context, and past workarounds. (This is not a "learning styles" quiz. The research doesn't support matching study methods to styles, so the profile is about what actually helps you and what gets in your way.)
2. Describe at least two ways their beliefs about ability (fixed vs. growth) shape their choices and persistence.
3. Apply growth-mindset reframing to a real academic setback.
4. Create at least three SMART goals for the coming weeks.
5. Explain the three phases of Zimmerman's self-regulation cycle and place themselves within it.
### Desired outcomes
 
- A written "About Me as a Learner" profile naming at least 2 strengths, 2 challenges, and 1 current workaround.
- One reframing exercise where a fixed "I can't" statement becomes "I can't yet, and here's the path."
- A SMART goals sheet with at least 3 goals, each with a metric, deadline, and first action step.
- A one-paragraph motivation statement ("why I'm here").
- A one-page Zimmerman cycle sheet for one real goal: forethought (goal and plan), performance (strategy and how you'll monitor), self-reflection (what worked, what to change).
### Start of week (10 min)
 
### Sessions
 
- Session 1 — The Learner Inventory (≈55 min)
- Session 2 — Mindset: Fixed vs. Growth (≈55 min)
- Session 3 — Motivation That Lasts (≈50 min)
- Session 4 — Goals & Self-Regulation (≈60 min)
### End-of-week self-check (20 min)

## Module 2 — Learning How to Learn: "Study in a Way That Sticks"
 
**Theme:** The craft. Replace low-yield habits (re-reading, highlighting) with high-yield, evidence-based ones.
 
**CLOs in play:** 4, 6
 
### Learning objectives
 
1. Analyze the difference between passive and active study, and rank common techniques by evidence.
2. Apply retrieval practice (self-testing) and spaced practice to a real study session.
3. Differentiate effective note-taking (Cornell, active annotation) from transcription.
4. Apply a structured reading routine (Survey–Question–Read–Recite–Review) to academic text.
5. Create a personal study-strategy toolkit matched to their Module 1 profile.
### Desired outcomes
 
- A completed self-test using retrieval, with a short reflection on what the test revealed.
- A spaced-practice schedule for one subject (for example, study on Day 1, 3, and 7, not one 4-hour block).
- One page of annotated notes using an active method (not transcription).
- A one-paragraph reading log showing you ran Survey–Question–Read–Recite–Review on a real passage.
- A Strategy Toolkit card listing your top 3 strategies with a specific use-case for each.
### Start of week (10 min)
 
### Sessions
 
- Session 1 — The Evidence (≈70 min)
- Session 2 — Retrieval & Spacing in Practice (≈65 min)
- Session 3 — Notes That Work (≈65 min)
- Session 4 — Reading Academic Text (≈65 min)
### End-of-week self-check (20 min)

## Module 3 — Time, Procrastination & Systems: "Make the Workload Manageable"
 
**Theme:** The machinery. Turn intent into a repeatable weekly operating system, and handle the moments when you don't follow it.
 
**CLOs in play:** 5, 7
 
### Learning objectives
 
1. Analyze where their time actually goes (a one-week time audit).
2. Apply a weekly planning routine that front-loads commitments and protects focus time.
3. Explain procrastination as largely an emotion-regulation problem and apply at least two strategies for getting started.
4. Break large tasks into sequenced, manageable steps with checkpoints, and prepare for an exam or assignment by working backward from the date.
5. Evaluate common productivity tools and choose one stack that fits their system (not the hype).
### Desired outcomes
 
- A completed one-week time audit showing real vs. perceived time use.
- A working weekly plan (one real week, not a fantasy week) with fixed commitments, blocked focus time, and a buffer.
- A short priority-triage note for a busy week (urgent vs. important vs. delegable or droppable).
- One large task broken down into at least 5 sequenced steps with interim checkpoints, plus one "if–then" start plan for the step you're most likely to avoid.
- An exam or assignment prep plan for one real upcoming deadline: worked backward from the date, with three spaced sessions, one practice test, and a post-exam or post-assignment review prompt.
- A one-line tool decision (one planner/calendar + one note system) with the reason it fits you.
### Start of week (10 min)
 
### Sessions
 
- Session 1 — The Time Audit (≈60 min, plus tracking all week)
- Session 2 — The Weekly Operating System, Triage & Tools (≈70 min)
- Session 3 — Procrastination, Big Tasks & Small Steps (≈70 min)
- Session 4 — Exams & Assignments Without Cramming (≈65 min)
### End-of-week self-check (20 min)

## Module 4 — Well-being, Resources & Sustainability: "Make It Last"
 
**Theme:** The fuel. Success that burns you out isn't success. Integrate well-being, resources, and persistence into a plan you'll actually keep.
 
**CLOs in play:** 8 (integrating all)
 
### Learning objectives
 
1. Explain the evidence link between sleep, stress, and academic performance.
2. Apply at least two evidence-based stress-regulation or recovery strategies.
3. Identify the resources most relevant to their situation (academic, health, financial, social).
4. Analyze persistence and "grit" as learned behaviors, not fixed traits, weigh the criticisms of the idea, and plan for likely setbacks.
5. Create a Personal Student Success Plan that integrates all four weeks.
### Desired outcomes
 
- A well-being check-in naming your sleep and stress baseline and two concrete recovery strategies you'll use.
- A resources map listing at least 3 relevant supports (academic advising or tutoring, health or counseling, financial, peer or community).
- A setback plan: "If I get a bad grade / a crisis / a low week, my first three moves are…"
- A persistence note: one past setback reframed, plus a one-sentence self-compassion script you can use next time.
- The capstone: a one- to two-page Personal Student Success Plan integrating mindset, study strategy, weekly system, and well-being, with a 30/60/90-day view (template at the end of this document).
### Start of week (10 min)
 
### Sessions
 
- Session 1 — The Body in the Room (≈65 min)
- Session 2 — Regulation That Works (≈60 min)
- Session 3 — You Don't Have to Do It Alone (≈60 min)
- Session 4 — Persistence & the Capstone (≈90 min)
### End-of-week self-check (20 min)

## Capstone template: Personal Student Success Plan (1–2 pages)
 
1. **My motivation (2–3 sentences).** Why I'm here, in my own words. Sample start: "I'm doing this because…"
2. **How I learn (Module 1).** My top 2 strengths, 2 challenges, and the workaround I trust most. One growth reframe I'll use when I get stuck.
3. **How I study (Module 2).** My top 3 strategies, each with a use-case. Sample: "Retrieval practice: after each reading, close the book and write what I remember."
4. **How I run my week (Module 3).** My planner and note system, my weekly plan skeleton (fixed commitments, focus blocks, buffer), my Sunday reset time, and my first move when I catch myself procrastinating (my if–then plan).
5. **How I stay well (Module 4).** My sleep target, my two recovery strategies, my early warning signs, and my top 3 supports with how to use each.
6. **If things go wrong.** My first three moves after a bad grade, a crisis, or a low week.
7. **30 / 60 / 90 days.**
   | | Focus | Instructions |
   |---|---|---|
   | **Day 30** | Make the routines stick | "Do the Sunday reset 4 weeks in a row. Complete one full Day 1 / 3 / 7 spaced cycle for [subject]. Keep the sleep log going." |
   | **Day 60** | Test and adjust | "Run one exam or assignment prep plan end to end and write the post-mortem. Cut one low-value habit. Check in with my accountability partner." |
   | **Day 90** | Audit and rewrite | "Repeat the time audit. Compare with Week 3. Rewrite this plan, keeping what worked." |
8. **Accountability.** Who will I share this with, and when will I check in?
