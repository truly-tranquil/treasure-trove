# Session 2 — Retrieval, Spacing & Interleaving in Practice (≈65 min)

*Optional extras add about 25 minutes: a 20-minute reading and a 5-minute partner step, plus an optional podcast episode.*

**Focus:** how to self-test, build a spaced schedule, and mix practice. **CLOs:** 4.

**You'll Make:** retrieval reflection and spaced-practice schedule ([Handout 2-B](materials.md#handout-2b)).

---

## Warm-up from memory (5 min)

Name the two high-utility techniques and one low-utility technique from Session 1. Then check your notes.

---

## Core ideas (10 min)

- **Retrieval practice.** Pull information out of memory before you look at it: blank-page recall, flashcards, practice questions, explaining aloud with the book closed. Check afterward and mark the gaps. The gaps tell you what to study next.
- **Spaced practice.** Take the same study time and spread it across days. A Day 1 / Day 3 / Day 7 pattern beats one long block. The benefit is largest when you need to remember for weeks or months, so work back from your exam date and lengthen the gaps as you go.
- **Interleaving.** Mix different topics or problem types in one session instead of doing one kind for hours. It feels harder and slower, and it builds the skill of choosing the right method.
- **Low-stakes and low-tech.** Paper flashcards work as well as an app. Feedback matters, so always check what you recalled.

!!! note "Why interleaving isn't in Session 1's top two"
    In the Dunlosky review you read in Session 1, practice testing and distributed practice earned the "high" rating, and interleaved practice earned "moderate." That isn't a contradiction. Interleaving is a promising technique with a narrower evidence base, so treat it as a useful add-on to retrieval and spacing, not a replacement for them.

## Learn (20 min)

- **Skim (15 min):** the Learning Scientists' [posters](https://www.learningscientists.org/posters) on spaced practice, retrieval practice, and interleaving. Each one is a one-page summary of the strategy and how to use it.
- **Download (5 min):** the free [bookmarks](https://www.learningscientists.org/bookmarks) (a handy reminder of the six strategies while you study).
- **Optional listen (17 min):** Learning Scientists Podcast, [Episode 1](https://www.learningscientists.org/learning-scientists-podcast/2017/9/5/episode-1-welcome-to-the-learning-scientists-podcast), an overview of all six strategies. Listen if you'd like the big picture, and skip it if you're short on time.
- **Optional read (20 min):** Weinstein, Madan & Sumeracki, ["Teaching the science of learning,"](https://nottingham-repository.worktribe.com/output/906972/teaching-the-science-of-learning) *Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications*. An open-access overview of all six strategies, including the spacing research. Skim the sections on the strategies you want to try.

---

## Do (30 min)

**Solo retrieval drill (15 min).** Pick one real topic you studied recently. Close everything, set a 5-minute timer, and write everything you remember on a blank page. Then check against your source and mark what you missed. Write two sentences on what the drill revealed.

**Spaced schedule (10 min).** Build a Day 1 / Day 3 / Day 7 plan for one real subject on [Handout 2-B](materials.md#handout-2b). If you have an exam date, work backward from it.

**Interleave one session (5 min).** Plan one study block that mixes two topics or problem types, and write it into the schedule.

**Optional partner step (5 min).** Quiz each other from your notes, then trade schedules and ask, "Where could you add a gap?"
