# Module 2 — Learning How to Learn

*"Study in a Way That Sticks"*

**Week 2 of 4** · CLOs 4 and 6

Replace low-yield habits like re-reading and highlighting with high-yield, evidence-based ones. Every strategy you meet this week is something you'll try on real material and then judge by how it worked. Expect it to feel harder than re-reading. That difficulty is part of why it works.

A no-tech version is always available: paper, a pen, and a timer.

### Start of week · 10 min

- Rate your confidence from 1 (not at all) to 5 (very) on each of the five items under "What you'll be able to do" below, and write the numbers down. You'll re-rate at the end of the week.
- Open your [Handout 1-A](../module-1/materials.md#handout-1a) and circle the one challenge you most want to fix. Everything this week is aimed at it.
- Write down the study technique you use most, and guess whether it is passive or active.

### End-of-week wrap-up · 30 min { #wrap-up }

**Build your Strategy Toolkit card (15 min).** Using the challenge you circled at the start of the week, choose your top three strategies from this week and complete [Handout 2-D](materials.md#handout-2d). Be specific about when you will use each.

**Self-check (15 min).** Answer from memory first, then check your notes and the answers.

1. Name two high-utility and two low-utility techniques from the Dunlosky review.
2. Why does re-reading feel effective but work poorly?
3. What does a Day 1 / 3 / 7 schedule look like for one of your subjects?
4. What are the three parts of a Cornell page?
5. Name the five steps of SQ3R.

??? note "Answers"

    1. High: practice testing, distributed practice. Low: highlighting, re-reading, summarization, keyword mnemonic, imagery for text.
    2. It makes material look familiar, which feels like learning.
    3. Study on Day 1, retrieve on Day 3, retrieve again on Day 7.
    4. Notes area, cue column, summary strip.
    5. Survey, Question, Read, Recite, Review.

Then re-rate your confidence on the five items and compare with the start of the week.

## What you'll be able to do

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

1. Analyze the difference between passive and active study, and rank common techniques by evidence.
2. Apply retrieval practice (self-testing), spaced practice, and interleaving to a real study session.
3. Differentiate effective note-taking (Cornell, active annotation) from transcription.
4. Apply a structured reading routine (Survey–Question–Read–Recite–Review) to academic text.
5. Create a personal study-strategy toolkit matched to your Week 1 profile.

## What you'll have by the end

- [ ] A completed self-test using retrieval, with a short reflection on what the test revealed
- [ ] A spaced-practice schedule for one real subject (for example, study on Day 1, 3, and 7, not one 4-hour block), including one interleaved study block
- [ ] One page of annotated notes made with an active method (not transcription)
- [ ] A one-paragraph reading log showing you ran Survey–Question–Read–Recite–Review on a real passage
- [ ] A **Strategy Toolkit card** listing your top 3 strategies, each with a specific use-case and the challenge it targets

The Strategy Toolkit card and spaced schedule feed into your [Personal Student Success Plan](../capstone.md).

## Facilitator notes (if you run this live)

- Open Session 1 with a quick poll: which technique do students use most, and is it passive or active? Reveal the ratings afterward.
- In Session 2, run the retrieval drill together with a timer, then swap pages for peer checking. Debrief on how it felt versus how it went.
- In Session 3, demonstrate Cornell notes on a short live clip and have students add cue questions in pairs.
- In Session 4, assign one shared short reading and run SQ3R together, then compare margin questions.
- Start each session with the warm-up questions, and run the end-of-week self-check as a short quiz.
- If you keep a grade, use completion of the four handouts plus the self-check rather than participation points.
