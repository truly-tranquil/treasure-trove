# Module 2 — Learning How to Learn

*"Study in a Way That Sticks"*

**Week 2 of 4** · CLOs 4 and 6 (course learning objectives, listed in the [syllabus](../syllabus.md))

This week you swap low-yield habits like re-reading and highlighting for methods the research supports. You'll try each one on material you're studying and judge it by how it went. Expect it to feel harder than re-reading. That extra effort is part of why it works.

[Start Session 1](session-1.md){ .md-button .md-button--primary }

---

## What you'll be able to do

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

1. Explain the difference between passive and active study, and rank common techniques by evidence.
2. Use retrieval practice (self-testing), spaced practice, and interleaving in a real study session.
3. Tell effective note-taking (Cornell, active annotation) from transcription.
4. Read academic text with a structured routine (Survey–Question–Read–Recite–Review).
5. Build a personal study-strategy toolkit that matches your Week 1 profile.

## Your week

About 5 hours of core work: a 10-minute start-of-week check, four sessions (70 + 65 + 65 + 60 minutes), and a 30-minute end-of-week wrap-up. Optional extras add about 35 minutes across the week, plus an optional podcast episode.

| Item | Details |
|------|---------|
| **Theme** | Passive vs. active study, retrieval, spacing, interleaving, notes, and reading |
| **CLOs** | 4 (Learn how to learn), 6 (Read and process academic text) |
| **Capstone** | Feeds section 3 of your [Personal Student Success Plan](../capstone.md): the Strategy Toolkit card and spaced-practice schedule. The notes page and reading log are practice. |
| **Works with** | Alone, with a study buddy, or [live with a group](../facilitator-guide.md#module-2-learning-how-to-learn) |

Each session opens with a 5-minute warm-up from memory. Then come core ideas and a reading, video, or listen with a link and time estimate, and an activity you can do alone or, in most sessions, with an optional partner step. Everything also works with paper, a pen, and a timer.

### Start of week · 10 min

- Rate your confidence from 1 (not at all) to 5 (very) on each of the five items above, and write the numbers down. You'll re-rate at the end of the week.
- Open your [Handout 1-A](../module-1/materials.md#handout-1a) and circle the one challenge you most want to fix. Everything this week is aimed at it.
- Write down the study technique you use most, and guess whether it is passive or active.

### End-of-week wrap-up · 30 min { #wrap-up }

**Build your Strategy Toolkit card (15 min).** Using the challenge you circled at the start of the week, choose your top three strategies from this week and complete [Handout 2-D](materials.md#handout-2d). Be specific about when you will use each.

**Self-check (15 min).** Answer from memory first, then check your notes and the answers.

1. Name two high-utility and two low-utility techniques from the Dunlosky review.
2. Why does re-reading feel effective but work poorly?
3. What does a Day 1 / 3 / 7 schedule look like for one of your subjects?
4. What are the three parts of a Cornell page?
5. Name the five steps of SQ3R.

??? note "Answers"

    1. High: practice testing, distributed practice. Low: highlighting, re-reading, summarization, keyword mnemonic, imagery for text.
    2. It makes material look familiar, which feels like learning. But recognizing something isn't the same as being able to recall it.
    3. Study on Day 1, retrieve on Day 3, retrieve again on Day 7.
    4. Notes area, cue column, summary strip.
    5. Survey, Question, Read, Recite, Review.

Then re-rate your confidence on the five items and compare with the start of the week.
