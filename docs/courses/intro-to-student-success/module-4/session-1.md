# Session 1 — The Body in the Room (≈65 min)

*Optional extras add about 5 minutes: a partner step.*

**Focus:** sleep, stress, and performance; noticing your own early warning signs. **CLOs:** 8.

**Deposit:** sleep and stress log, early warning signs ([Handout 4-A](materials.md#handout-4a)).

## Warm-up from memory (5 min)

From Week 3, without looking: name the three layers of your weekly plan. Then check [Handout 3-B](../module-3/materials.md#handout-3b).

## Core ideas (10 min)

- **Sleep is part of studying.** Memory, attention, and mood all depend on sleep. Cutting sleep to study more often costs more than it gains, especially for the retrieval and spacing strategies you built in Week 2.
- **Stress isn't the enemy.** Short-term, manageable stress can help you perform. Chronic, unrelieved stress wears down sleep, focus, mood, and health. The goal is recovery, not zero stress.
- **Sleep hygiene basics.** A steady sleep and wake time, a wind-down routine, a dark and cool room, limited caffeine late in the day, and screens away before bed. Small changes count.
- **Early warning signs.** Burnout rarely arrives at once. Look for your own early signals: sleeping badly, irritability, dreading tasks you used to handle, procrastinating more, skipping meals, pulling away from people. Knowing yours lets you act sooner.
- **Know your baseline.** You can't notice a change unless you know what "normal" looks like for you.
- **Not everything is fixable with habits.** If poor sleep or high stress persists, or you notice persistent low mood or anxiety, that is a reason to talk to a professional, not a personal failing. [Session 3](session-3.md) covers how, and the [Get Help](../../../get-help.md) page lists places to start.

## Learn (15 min)

- **Read (15 min):** Cornell Health, ["Sleep."](https://health.cornell.edu/resources/health-topics/sleep) It covers how much students need, how sleep affects academic performance, sleep hygiene tips, and a free self-guided program called Refresh. Skim for what applies to you.

For most adults, sleep needs fall in the range of roughly 7–9 hours. For teens, it's roughly 8–10 hours. Use the Cornell page to set your own target.

---

## Do (35 min)

1. **Start a 7-day sleep and stress log (10 min to set up).** Use [Handout 4-A](materials.md#handout-4a). Each morning, record bedtime, wake time, and a sleep quality rating (1–5). Each evening, record a stress rating (1–5) and one note on what drove it. It takes about 2 minutes a day. Fill in your baseline from the start of the week first.
2. **List your early warning signs (15 min).** Think of a past time when you were overwhelmed. What did you notice first, a few weeks before it peaked? List at least three signs.
3. **Set a sleep target (10 min).** Write one realistic target (hours and a rough bedtime), and one change to try this week.

**Optional partner step (5 min).** Share one early warning sign with a buddy, and agree to check in once this week. Share only what you're comfortable sharing.

---

## Options

- **Paper grid, notes app, or a voice memo each evening.**
- **Low-tech floor.** Seven lines on a sheet of paper.
- **If tracking makes you anxious,** keep it to a simple daily one-line note. Logging can feel stressful for some people, and you can skip anything that doesn't feel right.
