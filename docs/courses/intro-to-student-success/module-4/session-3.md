# Session 3 — You Don't Have to Do It Alone (≈60 min)

*Optional extras add about 5 minutes: a partner step.*

**Focus:** finding and using real supports. **CLOs:** 8.

**Deposit:** resources map and setback plan ([Handout 4-C](materials.md#handout-4c)).

---

## Warm-up from memory (5 min)

Name your two recovery strategies and when you will use each. Then check [Handout 4-B](materials.md#handout-4b).

---

## Core ideas (10 min)

- **Using resources is a skill.** Strong students use support early and often. Asking for help is part of self-regulation.
- **Four kinds of support.** *Academic* (advising, tutoring, writing center, office hours, library), *health* (counseling, medical care, disability or accessibility services), *financial* (aid office, emergency funds, food pantry, work-study), and *peer or community* (study groups, clubs, family, mentors, online communities).
- **Start with a name and a first step.** A resource you can't describe isn't useful yet. For each one, write what it is, how to reach it, and one thing you'd ask.
- **Not enrolled anywhere?** Use the public library, community or adult-education tutoring, a local health clinic, and your own peer or online study community. These count in full.
- **Plan for setbacks before they happen.** A setback plan is a short set of pre-decided moves for a bad grade, a crisis, or a low week. Deciding in advance is easier than deciding in the middle of it.
- **Connect to Week 1.** Relatedness is one of the three needs in Self-Determination Theory (see [Handout 1-C](../module-1/materials.md#handout-1c)). Support is part of your motivation system.

---

## Where to look

There's no external reading this session. The work is finding your own local resources. These two pages are places to start:

- **[Get Help](../../../get-help.md):** crisis lines, ways to find a therapist, and free or low-cost wellness tools. Many of them are US-based, and international options are listed where known.
- **[Learning Library](../../../learning-library.md):** free tutoring, writing help, open textbooks, and adult-education resources.

Beyond those, try your school or program website, a student handbook, your public library, and a web search for your area.

---

## Do (45 min)

**Resource scavenger.** Use [Handout 4-C](materials.md#handout-4c).

1. **Search (25 min).** Find your top 3 real supports, with at least one from a different category than the others. Write the name, how to reach it, and when it's available.
2. **Write one line for each (10 min):** "I'll use ___ when ___ by ___."
3. **Draft your setback plan (10 min).** Write your first three moves for a bad grade, a crisis, and a low week. Fill in the line about who or what you'll contact if you ever feel unsafe or in crisis (in the US, 988 is a crisis line you can call or text).

**Optional partner step (5 min).** Swap lists and ask, "Which of these would you hesitate to use, and what would make it easier?" Share only what you're comfortable sharing.

---

## Options

- **Written, a spreadsheet, or index cards.**
- **Privacy.** Keep your handout somewhere you can find it, and share only what you want to.
- **Cost.** Many campus services are free to enrolled students. If cost is a barrier, ask a financial aid office, a community health center, or a library for options.
