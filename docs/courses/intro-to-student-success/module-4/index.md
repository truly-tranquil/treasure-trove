!!! note "A note on care"
    This week touches on sleep, stress, and mental health. It is educational, not medical or clinical advice. Share only what you want to share, and skip any prompt that doesn't feel right. If you are struggling, Session 3 helps you find real support near you, and the [Get Help](../../../get-help.md) page lists crisis lines and ways to find a therapist.

    **If you are in crisis or thinking about harming yourself, contact a crisis line right away. In the US, call or text 988 (the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), or call 911 in an emergency. Outside the US, contact your local emergency number or crisis service.**

### Start of week · 10 min

- Rate your confidence from 1 (not at all) to 5 (very) on each of the five items under "What you'll be able to do" below, and write the numbers down. You'll re-rate at the end of the week.
- Write your **average sleep hours** and **stress level (1–5)** for the past week. A rough guess is fine. This is your baseline for [Handout 4-A](materials.md#handout-4a).
- Gather the things you made: Handouts [1-A](../module-1/materials.md#handout-1a) through [1-D](../module-1/materials.md#handout-1d), your Week 2 [Strategy Toolkit card](../module-2/materials.md#handout-2d) and [spaced schedule](../module-2/materials.md#handout-2b), and your Week 3 weekly plan, if–then plan, and prep plan. You'll use all of them in Session 4. If you've already drafted sections of your plan along the way, bring those too. The [capstone page](../capstone.md) shows which sections you can draft early.

### End-of-week self-check · 15 min

Answer from memory first, then check your notes and the answers.

1. What are three sleep hygiene habits that help?
2. Name two stress-recovery strategies you'll use and when.
3. Who are your first three supports?
4. What are your first three moves after a bad grade?
5. What is one criticism of the grit idea?

??? note "Answers"

    1. Your own. Examples are a steady sleep schedule, a wind-down routine, limiting late caffeine, and keeping screens away before bed.
    2. Your own, tied to a trigger (for example, a self-compassion break when you notice a warning sign).
    3. Your own, from Handout 4-C.
    4. Your own, from Handout 4-C.
    5. Later reviews question how distinct grit is from conscientiousness, and the idea can understate the role of circumstances.

Then re-rate your confidence on this week's five items. If you kept your ratings from the earlier modules, look back over all four and see how they changed.

## What you'll be able to do

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

1. Explain the evidence link between sleep, stress, and academic performance.
2. Apply at least two evidence-based stress-regulation or recovery strategies.
3. Identify the resources most relevant to your situation (academic, health, financial, social).
4. Analyze persistence and "grit" as learned behaviors, not fixed traits, weigh the criticisms of the idea, and plan for likely setbacks.
5. Create a Personal Student Success Plan that integrates all four weeks.

## What you'll have by the end

- [ ] A well-being check-in naming your sleep and stress baseline and two concrete recovery strategies you'll use
- [ ] A resources map listing at least 3 relevant supports (academic advising or tutoring, health or counseling, financial, peer or community)
- [ ] A setback plan: "If I get a bad grade / a crisis / a low week, my first three moves are…"
- [ ] A persistence note: one past setback reframed, plus a one-sentence self-compassion script you can use next time
- [ ] **The capstone:** a one- to two-page [Personal Student Success Plan](../capstone.md) integrating mindset, study strategy, weekly system, and well-being, with a 30/60/90-day view
