# Module 4 Materials

Handouts for Module 4. Each one has a version you can read here and a copy you can fill in: make your own Google Doc, download a PDF or Word file, or print it. You can also copy the prompts into a notebook, or answer them out loud as a voice memo. Every handout lists its own options.

This module touches on sleep, stress, and mental health. It is educational, not medical or clinical advice. Share only what you want to share, and skip any prompt that doesn't feel right. If you are struggling, the [Get Help](../../../get-help.md) page lists crisis lines and ways to find support.

## Handout 4-A: Well-being Check-in { #handout-4a }

*Session 1 · about 35 minutes, plus about 2 minutes a day of logging*

You can't notice a change unless you know what "normal" looks like for you. This handout holds your baseline, a 7-day sleep and stress log, your early warning signs, and a sleep target. It is a working document, not a test.

**Get a copy:** [Google Doc](https://docs.google.com/document/d/REPLACE-WITH-DOC-ID-4A/copy) · [PDF](https://docs.google.com/document/d/REPLACE-WITH-DOC-ID-4A/export?format=pdf) · [Word](https://docs.google.com/document/d/REPLACE-WITH-DOC-ID-4A/export?format=docx)

### What goes on it

**My baseline (from the start of the week):** average sleep ___ hours · stress ___ / 5

**7-day log.** Each morning, fill in your bedtime, wake time, and sleep quality. Each evening, fill in your stress rating and what drove it.

| | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|-----|-----|-----|-----|-----|-----|-----|
| Bedtime / wake time |  |  |  |  |  |  |  |
| Sleep quality (1–5) |  |  |  |  |  |  |  |
| Stress (1–5) |  |  |  |  |  |  |  |
| Main driver of stress |  |  |  |  |  |  |  |

**My early warning signs (at least 3):** 1. … 2. … 3. …

**My sleep target:** … **One change I'll try this week:** …

### Notes

- **Sleep range.** For most adults, sleep needs fall in the range of roughly 7–9 hours. For teens, it's roughly 8–10 hours. Use the Cornell Health page in [Session 1](session-1.md) to set your own target.
- **Sensitivity.** Logging can feel stressful for some people. If tracking makes you anxious, keep it to a simple daily one-line note.
- **Low-tech floor.** Seven lines on a sheet of paper.

**Options:** a paper grid, a notes app, or a voice memo each evening.

??? example "Worked example"

    **My baseline:** average sleep 6 hours · stress 4 / 5

    | | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
    |---|-----|-----|-----|-----|-----|-----|-----|
    | Bedtime / wake time | 12:30 / 7:00 | 12:00 / 7:00 | 1:00 / 7:30 | 11:30 / 7:00 | 12:30 / 8:00 | 1:30 / 9:30 | 11:00 / 7:00 |
    | Sleep quality (1–5) | 2 | 3 | 2 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 4 |
    | Stress (1–5) | 4 | 4 | 5 | 3 | 3 | 2 | 3 |
    | Main driver of stress | Stats problem set | Work shift ran late | Two deadlines on one day | Group chat drama | Paper outline | Nothing in particular | Planning the week |

    **My early warning signs:** 1. I stop eating lunch. 2. I start avoiding texts from friends. 3. I dread tasks I normally don't mind.

    **My sleep target:** 7 hours, in bed by 11:30 p.m. on weeknights. **One change I'll try this week:** Charge my phone across the room so I'm not scrolling in bed.

## Handout 4-B: My Recovery Plan { #handout-4b }

*Session 2 · about 30 minutes*

Recovery is part of the system. This handout pairs two strategies (ideally one for the body and one for the mind) with the moment you'll use each, and gives you a stress reframe you've already rehearsed. Try each strategy once, notice what it does, and keep what helps.

**Get a copy:** [Google Doc](https://docs.google.com/document/d/REPLACE-WITH-DOC-ID-4B/copy) · [PDF](https://docs.google.com/document/d/REPLACE-WITH-DOC-ID-4B/export?format=pdf) · [Word](https://docs.google.com/document/d/REPLACE-WITH-DOC-ID-4B/export?format=docx)

### What goes on it

|  | Strategy 1 | Strategy 2 |
|---|------------|------------|
| What I'll do |  |  |
| Trigger (if I notice…) |  |  |
| How long it takes |  |  |
| What I noticed when I tried it |  |  |

**Stress reframe** (from your [Reframe Card, Handout 1-B](../module-1/materials.md#handout-1b)): Original: "…" → Growth version: "…"

### Notes

- **Be realistic.** If a strategy doesn't help the first time, try it again once or swap it.
- **When strategies aren't enough.** If stress, low mood, or anxiety is getting in the way of daily life, reach out to a counselor or doctor. That is a sign of good self-regulation, not weakness. The [Get Help](../../../get-help.md) page lists places to start.

**Options:** written, a voice memo, or a card kept in your planner.

??? example "Worked example"

    |  | Strategy 1 | Strategy 2 |
    |---|------------|------------|
    | What I'll do | A 10-minute walk outside | A 5-minute self-compassion break |
    | Trigger (if I notice…) | If I've read the same paragraph twice without taking it in | If I get a grade lower than I hoped |
    | How long it takes | 10 minutes | 5 minutes |
    | What I noticed when I tried it | I felt calmer and went back to the page more focused. | It felt awkward at first, but it stopped me from spiraling. |

    **Stress reframe:** Original: "I can't handle this." → Growth version: "I can't handle all of this at once yet. The first step is to write down the three due dates, and I'll ask my sister to check in on Thursday."

## Handout 4-C: Resources Map & Setback Plan { #handout-4c }

*Session 3 · about 45 minutes*

Using resources is a skill, and strong students use support early. This handout holds your top three real supports and a setback plan: the first three moves you'll make if a bad grade, a crisis, or a low week hits. Deciding in advance is easier than deciding in the middle of it.

**Get a copy:** [Google Doc](https://docs.google.com/document/d/REPLACE-WITH-DOC-ID-4C/copy) · [PDF](https://docs.google.com/document/d/REPLACE-WITH-DOC-ID-4C/export?format=pdf) · [Word](https://docs.google.com/document/d/REPLACE-WITH-DOC-ID-4C/export?format=docx)

### What goes on it

Find at least three real supports, with at least one from a different category than the others. Categories: academic, health, financial, or peer and community.

| Support | Category | How to reach it | How I'll use it (one line) |
|---------|----------|-----------------|----------------------------|
| 1 |  |  |  |
| 2 |  |  |  |
| 3 |  |  |  |

**Setback plan: my first three moves if…**

|  | Move 1 | Move 2 | Move 3 |
|---|--------|--------|--------|
| I get a bad grade |  |  |  |
| A crisis hits (health, money, family, housing) |  |  |  |
| I have a low week |  |  |  |

**If I ever feel unsafe or in crisis:** I will contact ___ (local emergency number, a crisis line such as 988 in the US, or a trusted person).

### Notes

- **Privacy.** Keep this handout somewhere you can find it, and share only what you want to.
- **Cost.** Many campus services are free to enrolled students. If cost is a barrier, ask a financial aid office, a community health center, or a library for options.
- **Not enrolled anywhere?** The public library, community or adult-education tutoring, a local health clinic, and your own peer or online study community all count in full. The [Learning Library](../../../learning-library.md) and [Get Help](../../../get-help.md) pages are good places to start.

**Options:** written, a spreadsheet, or index cards.

??? example "Worked example"

    | Support | Category | How to reach it | How I'll use it (one line) |
    |---------|----------|-----------------|----------------------------|
    | 1. Writing center | Academic | Drop-in hours, Tuesday to Thursday, on the college website | I'll go with a rough draft at least three days before a paper is due. |
    | 2. Community health clinic | Health | Call the front desk to ask about counseling | I'll call if a low week lasts more than two weeks. |
    | 3. My sister | Peer and community | Text, or a Sunday call | I'll tell her my plan and ask her to check in once a week. |

    **Setback plan:**

    |  | Move 1 | Move 2 | Move 3 |
    |---|--------|--------|--------|
    | I get a bad grade | Wait 24 hours before deciding what it means. | Write what I missed and why (knowledge, strategy, time, or stress). | Take it to the writing center or the instructor's office hours. |
    | A crisis hits | Tell my sister what's going on. | Call the clinic or 211 for local help. | Email my instructors to ask about extensions. |
    | I have a low week | Cut my plan down to the three most important tasks. | Use my recovery strategies and protect my sleep. | Check in with my sister, and call the clinic if it continues. |

    **If I ever feel unsafe or in crisis:** I will call or text 988, or call my sister.

## Handout 4-D: Persistence Note { #handout-4d }

*Session 4 · about 15 minutes*

Persistence is built, not born. This handout turns one past setback into information using your three-move reframe, and gives you a one-sentence self-compassion script for next time. Pick a real setback, not a made-up one. Choose one you're comfortable thinking about.

**Get a copy:** [Google Doc](https://docs.google.com/document/d/REPLACE-WITH-DOC-ID-4D/copy) · [PDF](https://docs.google.com/document/d/REPLACE-WITH-DOC-ID-4D/export?format=pdf) · [Word](https://docs.google.com/document/d/REPLACE-WITH-DOC-ID-4D/export?format=docx)

### What goes on it

**A past setback:** …

**Original story:** "…" → **Reframe (yet + next step + resource):** "…"

**One idea from the Duckworth talk I'd keep:** … **One I'd question:** …

**My self-compassion script (one sentence):** "…"

### Notes

- **Keep it short.** A few sentences for each part is enough.
- **Where it goes next.** Your reframe and script feed section 6 ("If things go wrong") of your [Personal Student Success Plan](../capstone.md).

**Options:** typed, handwritten, or a voice memo.

??? example "Worked example"

    **A past setback:** I failed my first statistics exam.

    **Original story:** "I'm just not a math person." → **Reframe:** "I haven't passed a statistics exam yet. I'll start with short practice-question sessions spread across the week, and I'll go to tutoring before the next one."

    **One idea from the Duckworth talk I'd keep:** Sticking with something over a long time matters as much as how quickly I pick it up. **One I'd question:** It doesn't say much about how circumstances, like work hours or money, make persistence harder.

    **My self-compassion script:** "This is hard right now, other students hit this too, and I'll take one next step."
