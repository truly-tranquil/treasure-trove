# Facilitator Guide

*STU-101 · Student Success 101*

This page is for anyone running the course live with a group, in a class, a study circle, or a community program. If you're taking the course on your own, you can skip it. Every activity already has a solo version.

The guide opens with notes that apply to every module, then gives short notes for each module's sessions.

---

## For every module

- **Start each session with the warm-up questions.** Sessions from Module 1, Session 2 onward open with a 5-minute "from memory" warm-up. Let learners answer before they check their notes.
- **Run the end-of-week self-check as a short quiz.** Learners answer from memory first, then check the answer key. The check is for them, not for a grade.
- **Make every share optional.** Partner steps are always optional, and learners choose what to show. Offer a way to pass on any prompt without explaining why.
- **Go first, at a low stake.** When you share your own example (a time leak, a fixed belief, a warning sign), pick something real but small. It lowers the stakes for everyone else.
- **Know your crisis procedures before you start.** Modules 1 and 4 touch on identity, stress, and mental health. Learn your institution's or program's counseling and crisis procedures ahead of time and share them at the start of the course. The [Get Help](../../get-help.md) page lists crisis lines and ways to find a therapist, and you can point learners to it at any time.
- **Grade on completion, if you grade at all.** Use completion of each module's handouts plus the end-of-week self-check (and the capstone in Module 4). Don't give participation points, and never grade the content of personal disclosures.
- **Protect the solo and low-tech options.** Every module has a no-tech version: paper, a pen, and a timer. Learners who work without devices or without a partner are doing the course fully.
- **Allow extra time live.** The session times assume one person working alone. A group that shares, debriefs, and swaps pages will usually need more.

## Module 1 — Foundations & Mindset

[Module overview](module-1/index.md)

- **Session 1.** Remind learners that "course" means any class, training, certification, or project where they were learning. You don't need to be enrolled anywhere to take this course. Keep the partner step light: each person names one unexpected strength and shares only what they choose.
- **Session 2.** Before the reframe exercise, say plainly that growth-mindset language only helps when it changes what a person does. Model one reframe aloud with your own statement. The "When did this belief start?" question on the reframe card is optional, and some beliefs are tied to identity, disability, or past trauma. Never ask anyone to read a card aloud.
- **Session 3.** Motivation statements are personal. Invite learners to read theirs aloud to themselves, and make sharing with the group a choice. Point out that collective reasons (family, community, faith) are as valid as individual ones, and that a statement doesn't need to sound excited to be true.
- **Session 4.** In partner pairs, have each person ask one question per goal: "How will you measure that?" or "What might get in the way?" Watch for goals that are only outcomes, and prompt learners to add a process goal beside each.

## Module 2 — Learning How to Learn

[Module overview](module-2/index.md)

- **Session 1.** Open Session 1 with a quick poll: which technique do learners use most, and is it passive or active? Reveal the Dunlosky ratings afterward.
- **Session 2.** In Session 2, run the retrieval drill together with a timer, then swap pages for peer checking. Debrief on how it felt versus how it went. Expect learners to report that it felt harder than re-reading, and use that to explain why it works.
- **Session 3.** In Session 3, demonstrate Cornell notes on a short live clip, then have learners add cue questions in pairs.
- **Session 4.** In Session 4, assign one shared short reading and run SQ3R together, then compare margin questions.

---

## Module 3 — Time, Procrastination & Systems

[Module overview](module-3/index.md)

- **Session 1.** In Session 1, have learners write their predictions on a card and keep it sealed until the audit review in Session 4.
- **Session 2.** In Session 2, run the Eisenhower sort on a shared "mystery week" first, then have learners sort their own.
- **Session 3.** In Session 3, model your own if–then plan for something you avoid. Keep the tone warm, not preachy. If a learner describes avoidance across many tasks for weeks, or avoidance alongside low mood or anxiety, point them toward the supports in [Get Help](../../get-help.md) and Module 4.
- **Session 4.** In Session 4, have pairs stress-test each other's backward calendars.

## Module 4 — Well-being, Resources & Sustainability

[Module overview](module-4/index.md)

- **Session 1.** Open Session 1 by normalizing sleep and stress challenges. Share your own warning signs to lower the stakes, and make every share optional.
    - Have your counseling and crisis procedures ready before this module starts, and share them again at the beginning of Session 1.
- **Session 2.** In Session 2, lead the self-compassion break live, and let learners pass on any practice.
- **Session 3.** In Session 3, bring a list of your campus or community resources, and have learners search for local ones. Check that every resource on your list is current before the session. Learners who aren't enrolled anywhere can use the public library, community tutoring, a local clinic, and online study communities. The [Learning Library](../../learning-library.md) is a good place to start.
- **Session 4.** In Session 4, give learners the full 90 minutes for the capstone. Have pairs read plans aloud and ask the "which part is the hardest to keep?" question.
