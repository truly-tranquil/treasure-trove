# Facilitator Guide

*STU-101 · Student Success 101*

This page is for anyone running the course live with a group: a class, a study circle, or a community program. If you're taking the course on your own, you can skip it. Every activity already has a solo version.

It starts with notes that apply to every module, then gives short notes for each module's sessions.

---

## For every module

- **Open with the warm-up.** From Module 1, Session 2 onward, each session starts with a 5-minute "from memory" warm-up. Let people answer before they check their notes.
- **Run the self-check as a short quiz.** Learners answer from memory first, then check the answer key. It's for them, not for a grade.
- **Make every share optional.** Partner steps are always optional, and learners choose what to show. Give people a way to pass on any prompt without explaining why.
- **Go first, with something small.** When you share your own example (a time leak, a fixed belief, a warning sign), pick something true but low-stakes. It makes it easier for everyone else.
- **Know your crisis procedures before you start.** Modules 1, 3, and 4 touch on identity, stress, and mental health. Find out your institution's or program's counseling and crisis procedures ahead of time and share them at the start of the course. The [Get Help](../../get-help.md) page lists crisis lines and ways to find a therapist, and you can point people to it at any time.
- **Treat the solo and low-tech options as full participation.** Every module has a version that needs only paper, a pen, and a timer. Someone working without a device or a partner is doing the course in full.
- **Allow extra time.** Session times assume one person working alone. A group that shares, debriefs, and swaps pages will usually need more.
- **Space the sessions out.** Each module is built as one week. Meeting once to, if you can swing it four, times a week works better than four sessions back to back, because the habits (logs, reflections, spaced practice) need days between sessions to happen.

## Module 1 — Foundations & Mindset

[Module overview](module-1/index.md)

- **Session 1.** Remind people that "course" means any class, training, certification, or project where they were learning. They don't need to be enrolled anywhere. Keep the partner step light: each person names one unexpected strength and shares only what they choose.
- **Session 2.** Before the reframe exercise, say plainly that growth-mindset language only helps when it changes what a person does. Model one reframe aloud with your own statement. The "When did this belief start?" question on the card is optional, because some beliefs are tied to identity, disability, or past trauma. Never ask anyone to read a card aloud.
- **Session 3.** Motivation statements are personal. Invite people to read theirs aloud to themselves, and make sharing with the group a choice. Point out that reasons like family, community, and faith count as much as individual ones, and that a statement doesn't need to sound excited to be true.
- **Session 4.** In pairs, each person asks one question per goal: "How will you measure that?" or "What might get in the way?" Watch for goals that are only outcomes, and prompt people to add a process goal beside each.

## Module 2 — Learning How to Learn

[Module overview](module-2/index.md)

- **Session 1.** Start with a quick poll: which study technique does everyone use most, and is it passive or active? Reveal the Dunlosky ratings afterward.
- **Session 2.** Run the retrieval drill together with a timer, then swap pages so people can check each other's recall. Debrief on how it felt versus how it went. Many people will say it felt harder than re-reading, which is a good way into why it works.
- **Session 3.** Demonstrate Cornell notes on a short live clip, then have people add cue questions in pairs.
- **Session 4.** Pick one short reading for everyone, run SQ3R on it together, and compare margin questions.

## Module 3 — Time, Procrastination & Systems

[Module overview](module-3/index.md)

- **Scheduling.** The time audit runs all week, and Session 2 uses the days people have logged so far. Meet for Session 1 at the start of the week, hold Session 2 at least two or three days later, and save Session 4 for when everyone has close to a full week of data.
- **Session 1.** Have people write their predictions on Handout 3-A before they start logging, and ask them not to change them afterward. The comparison happens in Session 4.
- **Session 2.** Run the Eisenhower sort on a shared "mystery week" first, then let people sort their own tasks.
- **Session 3.** Model your own if–then plan for something you avoid, and keep the tone warm, not preachy. If someone describes avoiding many tasks for weeks, or avoidance alongside low mood or anxiety, point them to [Get Help](../../get-help.md) and Module 4.
- **Session 4.** Have pairs stress-test each other's backward calendars.

## Module 4 — Well-being, Resources & Sustainability

[Module overview](module-4/index.md)

- **Before you start.** Have your counseling and crisis procedures ready, and share them again at the beginning of Session 1.
- **Session 1.** Start by saying that sleep and stress problems are common. Share one of your own early warning signs, and make every share optional. The 7-day log begins here, so schedule Session 1 early enough in the week for people to finish it.
- **Session 2.** Lead the self-compassion break live, and let people pass on any practice.
- **Session 3.** Bring a list of campus or community resources and have people search for local ones. Check that everything on your list is current before the session. People who aren't enrolled anywhere can use the public library, community tutoring, a local clinic, and online study communities. The [Learning Library](../../learning-library.md) is a good place to start.
- **Session 4.** Give people the full 90 minutes for the capstone. Offer pairs the option to read their plans aloud and ask, "Which part is the hardest to keep?"
