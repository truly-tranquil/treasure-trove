# Module 1 — Foundations & Mindset

*"Who Am I as a Learner?"*

**Week 1 of 4** · CLOs 1–3

Before technique, identity. You can't regulate learning you haven't examined, so this week is about getting to know yourself as a learner: what helps, what gets in your way, what you believe about your own ability, and what you're working toward.

### End-of-week self-check · 20 min

Answer from memory first (15 min), then check your notes and the answers.

1. Name the three phases of Zimmerman's cycle and one thing you would do in each.
2. What are the three needs in Self-Determination Theory?
3. Rewrite "I'm just bad at this" using the three-move reframe.
4. What makes a goal SMART, and what is the difference between a process goal and an outcome goal?
5. What is a "false growth mindset"?

??? note "Answers"

    1. Forethought: plan and anticipate obstacles. Performance: do it and monitor. Self-reflection: compare to the goal and adjust.
    2. Autonomy, competence, relatedness.
    3. Add "yet," name a next step, connect to a resource.
    4. Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound. A process goal is the behavior you'll do, and an outcome goal is the result you want.
    5. Using growth-mindset language without changing what you do.

Then refine your Handout 1-A profile (5 min): add or change at least one entry based on what you learned this week. Finally, re-rate your confidence on the five items above and compare with the start of the week.

## What you'll be able to do

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

1. Identify your own learning profile: strengths, challenges, context, and past workarounds. (This is not a "learning styles" quiz. The research doesn't support matching study methods to styles, so the profile is about what actually helps you and what gets in your way.)
2. Describe at least two ways your beliefs about ability (fixed vs. growth) shape your choices and persistence.
3. Apply growth-mindset reframing to a real academic setback.
4. Create at least three SMART goals for the coming weeks.
5. Explain the three phases of Zimmerman's self-regulation cycle and place yourself within it.

## What you'll have by the end

- [ ] A written "About Me as a Learner" profile naming at least 2 strengths, 2 challenges, and 1 current workaround
- [ ] One reframing exercise where a fixed "I can't" statement becomes "I can't yet, and here's the path"
- [ ] A SMART goals sheet with at least 3 goals, each with a metric, deadline, and first action step
- [ ] A one-paragraph motivation statement ("why I'm here")
- [ ] A one-page Zimmerman cycle sheet for one real goal: forethought (goal and plan), performance (strategy and how you'll monitor), self-reflection (what worked, what to change)

These all feed into your [Personal Student Success Plan](../capstone.md).
