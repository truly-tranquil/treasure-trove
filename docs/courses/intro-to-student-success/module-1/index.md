**How every session works.** Each session follows the same six steps so you always know what comes next:
 
1. A 5-minute warm-up from memory (from Session 2 on)
2. The core ideas
3. A reading or video with a link and time estimate
4. The activity, with a solo version and an optional partner version
5. A deposit toward your capstone project
6. Options for how to do it (written, audio, or letter)
A no-tech version is always available: paper, a pen, and a timer. Every handout ends with a worked example so you can see what "specific enough" looks like.
 
**Who this is for.** This course works whether you are in school, in job training, or learning on your own. When a prompt says "course," read it as any class, training, certification, or project where you were learning something. You don't need to be enrolled anywhere to take it.
 
## Desired outcomes
 
- A written "About Me as a Learner" profile naming at least 2 strengths, 2 challenges, 1 helpful or harmful condition, and 1 current workaround, plus your "why" in one sentence and one thing you're curious about.
- One reframe card where a fixed "I can't" statement becomes "I can't yet, and here's the path."
- A rating of your autonomy, competence, and relatedness, one small step to strengthen your weakest need, and a one-paragraph motivation statement ("why I'm here").
- A SMART goals sheet with at least 3 goals, each with a metric, a deadline, and a first action step (one goal developed in full).
- A Zimmerman placement for each goal (forethought, performance, or self-reflection) and a description of the next loop for your main goal.
- A one-page Zimmerman cycle sheet for one real goal: forethought (goal and plan), performance (strategy and how you'll monitor), and self-reflection (what worked, what to change).
## Learning objectives
 
By the end of this week, you will be able to:
 
1. Identify your own learning profile: strengths, challenges, the conditions that help or hurt, and workarounds you already use.
2. Describe at least two ways your beliefs about ability (fixed vs. growth) shape your choices and persistence.
3. Apply growth-mindset reframing to a real setback.
4. Create at least three SMART goals for the next 4–12 weeks (one developed in full), and tell a process goal from an outcome goal.
5. Explain the three phases of Zimmerman's self-regulation cycle and place each of your goals within it.
## Start of week (10 min)
 
Rate your confidence from 1 (not at all) to 5 (very) on each of the five learning objectives above, and write the numbers down. You'll re-rate at the end of the week; the change is your evidence of progress.

## End-of-week self-check (20 min)
 
Answer from memory first (15 min), then check your notes and the answers below.
 
1. Name the three phases of Zimmerman's cycle and one thing you would do in each.
2. What are the three needs in Self-Determination Theory?
3. Rewrite "I'm just bad at this" using the three-move reframe.
4. What makes a goal SMART, and what is the difference between a process goal and an outcome goal?
5. What is a "false growth mindset"?

**Answers**
 
1. Forethought: plan and anticipate obstacles; Performance: do it and monitor; Self-reflection: compare to the goal and adjust.
2. Autonomy, competence, relatedness.
3. Add "yet," name a next step, connect to a resource.
4. Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound; a process goal is the behavior you'll do, an outcome goal is the result you want.
5. Using growth-mindset language without changing what you do.
Then refine your Handout 1-A profile (5 min): add or change at least one entry based on what you learned this week. Finally, re-rate your confidence on the five learning objectives and compare with the start of the week.

## Next steps & bridge to Week 2
 
- **Bridge to Week 2:** bring your learning profile with you. Week 2's study strategies (retrieval, spacing, SQ3R, active notes) will be chosen from your actual challenges, not assigned blindly. If you flagged "I cram and re-read," spacing comes first. If you flagged "I can't make sense of dense text," the reading routine comes first.
- **Return to Handout 1-E in Week 2:** once you've met a strategy you like, swap it in for your placeholder in the Performance box.
- **Habit to keep:** the 5-minute bedtime reflection and the weekly prompt.
- **Continued beyond:** revisit your motivation statement before every major deadline. It's your reset button.
