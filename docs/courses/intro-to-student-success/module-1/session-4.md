# Session 4 — Goals & Self-Regulation (≈65 min)

*Optional extras add about 35 minutes: a 30-minute reading and a 5-minute partner step.*

**Focus:** SMART goals; process vs. outcome goals; Zimmerman's self-regulation cycle; the weekly reflection prompt. **CLOs:** 3 (primary), 1 (applied).

**Deposit:** SMART goals sheet (3+ goals) with Zimmerman placement, and a Zimmerman cycle sheet for one goal.

---

## Warm-up from memory (5 min)

Name the three needs in Self-Determination Theory and say which one is weakest for you right now (check your [Handout 1-C](materials.md#handout-1c) rating). Then check your notes.

---

## Learn (15 min)

- **Read (10 min):** Purdue Global Academic Success Center, ["Teaching Students to Learn."](https://purdueglobalwriting.center/2016/08/10/teaching-students-to-learn/) A short, free overview of the three phases of self-regulation, with quotes from Zimmerman. It is written for faculty, so read it for the phases and examples.
- **Core idea (5 min): SMART goals, and process vs. outcome.** A SMART goal is:
    - **S**pecific (what exactly?)
    - **M**easurable (how will you know?)
    - **A**chievable (challenging but doable given your load)
    - **R**elevant (tied to your "why")
    - **T**ime-bound (it has a deadline)

    Goals also come in two kinds. An **outcome goal** is the result you want ("pass the certification exam"). A **process goal** is the behavior you control ("study three evenings a week"). Outcomes depend partly on things you can't control, so pair each outcome goal with a process goal. [Handout 1-D](materials.md#handout-1d) has a row for checking which kind you wrote.
- **Optional read (30 min):** Zimmerman (2002), ["Becoming a Self-Regulated Learner: An Overview"](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15430421tip4102_2) (*Theory Into Practice*).

---

## Do (45 min)

1. **Build your SMART goals sheet (20 min).** Choose three goals for the next 4–12 weeks. They don't all have to be academic: one can be well-being, one social, one a skill. Fill in every row for the goal you most want to keep (Goal 1). For Goals 2 and 3, fill in at least the metric, the deadline, and a first action step you can do within 48 hours; you can come back for the other rows later. Use [Handout 1-D](materials.md#handout-1d).
2. **Place each goal in the cycle (5 min).** For each goal, mark forethought, performance, or self-reflection. For Goal 1, write what the next loop looks like. (Most goals set this week are in forethought. That's fine.)
3. **Complete a Zimmerman cycle sheet for Goal 1 (10 min).** Fill in [Handout 1-E](materials.md#handout-1e). In the strategy box, a placeholder is fine (for example, "a timed 25-minute block"). Week 2 gives you real study strategies, and you'll swap one in then.
4. **Check your obstacle for a hidden belief (5 min).** Look at the obstacle you named on Handout 1-E. If it hides a fixed statement ("I'm just not a morning person," "I always lose motivation"), run it through a [Handout 1-B](materials.md#handout-1b) card: add "yet," name a next step, connect a resource. If it's purely practical (no quiet space, not enough time), skip the card and use the five minutes to sharpen your if–then.
5. **Set up your rhythms (5 min).** Put the weekly reflection prompt where you'll see it, and set a reminder for the 5-minute bedtime reflection starting tonight.

**Optional partner step (5 min).** Trade sheets and ask one question per goal: "How will you measure that?" or "What might get in the way?" Share only what you're comfortable sharing.
