## Handout 1-A: About Me as a Learner { #handout-1a }

*Session 1 · about 15 minutes to draft, plus 5 minutes to refine at the end of the week*

This is a working document, not a test. You’ll draft it in Session 1 and refine it in the end-of-week self-check. Sharing it with anyone is always optional.

**Download:** [PDF](../../../assets/intro%20to%20student%20success/handout-1-a-about-me-as-a-learner.pdf)

### What goes on it

1. **Two strengths**
2. **Two challenges**
3. **One condition that helps or hurts**, for example, “I focus better in the morning.”
4. **One (or more) workarounds I already use**
5. **My “why”** in one sentence. You’ll expand it in Session 3.
6. **One thing I’m curious about in how I learn**

**Options:** fill it in as a form, record a short voice memo walking through the six boxes, or write it as a letter to your future self. All count.

??? example "Worked example"

    This is one way it can look. Yours will be different, and rough is fine.

    | Section | Example entries |
    |---------|-----------------|
    | 1. Two strengths | a) I keep going once I’ve started. b) I ask good questions when something doesn’t make sense. |
    | 2. Two challenges | a) I put off the first step on big projects. b) I lose focus after about 20 minutes of reading. |
    | 3. One condition that helps or hurts | I focus better in the morning, before the house gets busy. |
    | 4. Workarounds I already use | a) I set a 25-minute timer. b) I read hard material out loud. |
    | 5. My “why” | I want to finish this certificate so I can change careers and feel proud of myself. |
    | 6. Curious about | Why do I remember things I hear better than things I read? |

## Handout 1-B: The Reframe Card { #handout-1b }

*Session 2 · about 12 minutes per card*

A fixed-mindset thought is a sentence that treats an ability as permanent (“I’m just not a ___ person”). This card turns it into a growth version in three small moves: add “yet,” name a next step, and connect a resource. Use a statement you actually say to yourself, not a made-up one.

**Download:** [PDF](../../../assets/intro%20to%20student%20success/handout-1-b-the-reframe-card.pdf)

### What goes on it

- **My fixed-mindset thought:** the “I can’t” or “I’m just not” sentence I really say.
- **1. Add “yet”:** “I can’t … yet.”
- **2. Next step:** “The first thing I’ll try is …”
- **3. Resource:** “I’ll use / ask / look up …”
- **Growth version (combined):** all three moves in one sentence.
- **When did this belief start?** Optional.

Keep your cards in your notebook or file system. You’ll reuse one in Session 4 (to check an obstacle for a hidden belief) and again in Week 4’s stress-regulation session.

**Sensitivity:** some fixed beliefs are tied to identity, disability, or past trauma. The origin question is optional, and you never have to share a statement with anyone.

**Options:** written, voice memo, or index card.

??? example "Worked example"

    | Step | Example card |
    |------|--------------|
    | My fixed-mindset thought | “I’m just not a numbers person.” |
    | 1. Add “yet” | “I can’t read a spreadsheet with confidence yet.” |
    | 2. Next step | “The first thing I’ll try is making a budget with just three numbers.” |
    | 3. Resource | “I’ll use a free beginner spreadsheet tutorial and ask a friend who uses spreadsheets at work one question.” |
    | Growth version (combined) | “I can’t read a spreadsheet with confidence yet, so I’ll start with a three-number budget and use a free tutorial when I get stuck.” |
    | When did this belief start? (optional) | “A math class where I got a bad grade and decided that was that.” |

## Handout 1-C: Motivation Statement Worksheet { #handout-1c }

*Session 3 · about 35 minutes, including writing and revising your paragraph*

**Self-Determination Theory (SDT)** says motivation lasts when three needs are met: **autonomy** (you have some choice), **competence** (you can grow), and **relatedness** (you feel connected to others). This worksheet helps you see which need is running low, then write a short statement of why you’re here.

**Download:** [PDF](../../../assets/intro%20to%20student%20success/handout-1-c-motivation-statement-worksheet.pdf)

### Part 1 — Rate your three needs

| Need | Rating (1–5) | One reason for this rating |
|------|--------------|----------------------------|
| Autonomy (I have some choice) | | |
| Competence (I can grow) | | |
| Relatedness (I’m connected to others) | | |

- **My weakest need right now:** …
- **One small thing that would strengthen it this week:** …

### Part 2 — Scaffolding prompts

Use these prompts as scaffolding, then integrate your answers into one paragraph (4–8 sentences) that you can revise.

- Why did I choose (or am I choosing) this path?
- What do I want this stretch to feel like on a good day?
- What do I want to avoid (exhaustion? the same frustration as last time?)?
- Who is on my team, even if it’s just “me, three months from now”?
- If I could only remember one sentence from this course, what would it be?

**Stuck?** Try the three SDT questions: (a) What do I want to choose? (b) What skill do I want to improve? (c) Who do I want to be around while I do this? Let your weakest need guide which one you start with.

**Cultural note:** not every “why” is individualistic. Collective motivations (family, community, faith) are fully valid expressions of relatedness. You don’t need a “grind-it-out” story, and your statement doesn’t have to sound excited to be true.

**Options:** a written paragraph, a 1-minute voice memo, or a 3-minute audio version where the three SDT questions are read aloud to you and you answer out loud. For the voice route, “revising” means recording a second take after you listen back.

??? example "Worked example"

    | Need | Rating (1–5) | One reason for this rating |
    |------|--------------|----------------------------|
    | Autonomy | 4 | I chose this certificate and I set my own study hours. |
    | Competence | 3 | I’m learning, but I don’t know yet if I can pass the exam. |
    | Relatedness | 2 | I’m studying alone and haven’t talked to anyone about it. |

    **Weakest need:** Relatedness.

    **One small thing that would strengthen it this week:** Tell one friend what I’m studying and ask if I can text them when I finish a session.

    **Sample statement (shortened):** “I’m doing this because I want a steadier job for my family, and because I’m tired of feeling stuck. On a good day this stretch feels calm and a little proud, not frantic. I want to avoid burning out the way I did last time. My team is my sister, who checks in on me, and me three months from now, who will be glad I kept going.”

## Handout 1-D: SMART Goals & Self-Regulation Sheet { #handout-1d }

*Session 4 · about 20 minutes*

There’s no “big enough” or “small enough.” The test is whether the goal is specific, measurable, and has a first step. Add rows if you want.

**Download:** [PDF](../../../assets/intro%20to%20student%20success/handout-1-d-smart-goals-self-regulation-sheet.pdf)

### How to use this sheet

Choose three goals for the next 4–12 weeks (they don’t all have to be academic). Fill in **Goal 1** first, and make it the goal you most want to keep, because you’ll take it to [Handout 1-E](#handout-1e).

### Two terms to know

- An **outcome goal** is the result you want (“pass the exam”).
- A **process goal** is the behavior you control (“study three evenings a week”).

Pair outcome goals with process goals whenever you can.

The three **Zimmerman phases** are **forethought** (planning it), **performance** (doing it and tracking it), and **self-reflection** (looking back and adjusting).

### What goes on it

For each of your three goals, answer:

- **S · What exactly?**
- **M · How will I measure it?**
- **A · Is it challenging but doable given my load?** Yes or no. If no, adjust it.
- **R · Why does it matter?** Link it to your motivation statement.
- **T · Deadline**
- **Process goal or outcome goal?** If it’s an outcome goal, what process goal goes with it?
- **First action (within 48 hours),** written as an if–then.
- **Where is this goal in the Zimmerman cycle?** Forethought, performance, or self-reflection.
- **My next loop looks like…** For Goal 1 only: what you’ll do in the next phase.

Most goals you set this week will be in forethought, and that’s fine.

??? example "Worked example (Goal 1)"

    | Question | Example |
    |---|---------|
    | S · What exactly? | Work through my certification study guide, one chapter at a time. |
    | M · How will I measure it? | Three 30-minute study sessions a week, ticked off on a wall calendar. |
    | A · Doable given my load? | Y. I have about 5 free hours a week, so three half-hour sessions fit. |
    | R · Why does it matter? | I want a steadier job for my family (from my motivation statement). |
    | T · Deadline | The exam date, six weeks from today. |
    | Process or outcome? | Process. It goes with my outcome goal, “pass the exam.” |
    | First action (if–then) | If it’s Tuesday after dinner, then I’ll open the study guide and read chapter 1 for 20 minutes. |
    | Zimmerman placement | Forethought. |
    | My next loop looks like… | Do the first week, track my ticks (performance), then check on Sunday how many sessions I really did (self-reflection). |

## Handout 1-E: Zimmerman Cycle Sheet (one goal) { #handout-1e }

*Session 4 · about 10 minutes*

Self-regulated learners move through a cycle of three phases, over and over. **Forethought** is what you do before you start (set the goal, make a plan, expect obstacles). **Performance** is what you do while you work (use a strategy, keep track of how it’s going). **Self-reflection** is what you do afterward (look back, then adjust). Each loop makes the next one better. The cycle is named for psychologist Barry Zimmerman.

**Download:** [PDF](../../../assets/intro%20to%20student%20success/handout-1-e-zimmerman-cycle-sheet-one-goal.pdf)

**Your goal:** pick the goal from [Handout 1-D](#handout-1d) that you most want to keep.

### What goes on it

**Forethought: goal, plan, and obstacle**

- My goal in one line
- My plan (when, where, how)
- One obstacle I expect
- My if–then for that obstacle

**Performance: strategy and monitoring**

- The strategy I’ll use. A placeholder is fine this week. In Week 2 you’ll meet real study strategies, so come back and swap one in.
- How I’ll check I’m on track (a count, a tick-box, a note)
- What I’ll do if I slip

**Self-reflection: evaluate and adjust**

- Date I’ll reflect
- What worked
- What didn’t
- One change for the next loop

### Two habits to keep

**Weekly reflection prompt.** Keep it at the bottom of your planner or in your phone notes:

1. What did I actually do?
2. How did it compare to the plan?
3. One adjustment for next week.

**Daily habit (starts tonight).** Spend 5 minutes before bed on three questions: What did I learn today? What got in the way? What’s one thing I’ll do differently tomorrow? Miss a night? Just pick it up the next one. Week 3’s Sunday reset grows out of the weekly prompt, so you only need these two rhythms.

**Options:** a typed sheet, a handwritten sheet, or a short voice walkthrough.

??? example "Worked example"

    **Goal (from Handout 1-D):** Three 30-minute study sessions a week for my certification exam.

    | Phase | Example |
    |-------|---------|
    | **Forethought** | **My goal in one line:** Do three 30-minute study sessions a week until the exam. **My plan:** Tuesday and Thursday after dinner, and Saturday morning, at the kitchen table. **One obstacle I expect:** I’ll be tired after work. **My if–then:** If I’m too tired to start, then I’ll do just 10 minutes and decide whether to keep going. |
    | **Performance** | **The strategy I’ll use:** A timed 25-minute block (placeholder until Week 2). **How I’ll check I’m on track:** A tick on the wall calendar after each session. **If I slip:** I’ll do a short session the next day instead of skipping two in a row. |
    | **Self-reflection** | **Date I’ll reflect:** Sunday evening. **What worked:** Saturday mornings. **What didn’t:** Thursday nights. **One change for the next loop:** Move Thursday’s session to lunchtime. |
