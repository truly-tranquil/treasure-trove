# Session 3 — Motivation That Lasts (≈55 min)

*Optional extras add about 30 minutes: a 15-minute skim and a 15-minute talk.*

**Focus:** intrinsic vs. extrinsic motivation; Self-Determination Theory (SDT); your motivation statement.

**Deposit:** SDT ratings and motivation statement (one paragraph).

---

## Warm-up from memory (5 min)

Write the three-move reframe from memory and apply it to "I can't focus." Then check [Handout 1-B](materials.md#handout-1b).

---

## Learn (15 min)

- **Read (15 min):** the Center for Self-Determination Theory's ["Theory" overview](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/theory/). Read the overview and the part on basic psychological needs (autonomy, competence, relatedness). It's written in plain language by the theory's home site.
- **Optional skim (15 min):** Ryan & Deci (2000), ["Self-Determination Theory and the Facilitation of Intrinsic Motivation, Social Development, and Well-Being."](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.55.1.68) The original research article. Read the introduction and the section on psychological needs and mental health, then skip the rest if you wish.
- **Optional watch (15 min):** Scott Geller, ["The psychology of self-motivation"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sxpKhIbr0E) (TEDxVirginiaTech).

---

## Do (35 min)

**Rate your three needs (5 min).** On [Handout 1-C](materials.md#handout-1c), rate your autonomy, competence, and relatedness in your current studies or work (1 = very low, 5 = very high), add one reason for each rating, circle your weakest need, and write one small thing that would strengthen it this week.

**Answer the scaffolding prompts (5 min).** Jot short answers to the five prompts on Handout 1-C.

**Write your motivation statement (15 min).** One paragraph, 4–8 sentences, answering: *Why am I here, and what do I want the next stretch to feel like?* You can mention family, community, faith, career, curiosity, a past failure you want to reverse, or a future version of yourself. Aim for honesty rather than impression: if a sentence sounds like something you'd say in a job interview, ask whether it's really true for you, and say it the way you'd say it to a friend. If you're stuck, use the three SDT questions on the handout:

- (a) What do I want to choose?
- (b) What skill do I want to improve?
- (c) Who do I want to be around while I do this?

Use your weakest need to guide you.

**Read it back (5 min).** Read it aloud to yourself (or a buddy, if you like). Does it sound like you, or like a cover letter? Revise once.

**Self-check (5 min).**

- Is it in my voice?
- Does it mention at least one specific thing (a course, a skill, a person, a goal)?
- Can I read it back and recognize myself in it?
