## Handout 3-A: Time Audit Summary { #handout-3a }

*Session 1 (set up) and Session 4 (finish) · about 10 minutes to set up, about 20 minutes to finish, plus about 5 minutes a day of tracking*

You can't manage what you haven't measured. This handout holds your prediction, your real log, and the gap between them. It is a working document, not a test. Make your prediction before you log anything, so it stays honest.

**Download:** [PDF](../../../assets/intro%20to%20student%20success/handout-3-a-time-audit-summary.pdf)

### What goes on it

**Your tracking method:** …

Write your prediction in hours for a typical week, then fill in "My log" in Session 4. Put whatever is left (meals, commuting, errands, downtime) under "Other," so each column adds up to 168 hours.

| Category | My prediction | My log | Gap (log minus prediction) |
|----------|---------------|--------|----------------------------|
| Classes or training |  |  |  |
| Study |  |  |  |
| Sleep |  |  |  |
| Work or caregiving |  |  |  |
| Screens (non-academic) |  |  |  |
| Other |  |  |  |
| **Total** | **168** | **168** | **0** |

**Three time leaks I found (at least 3):** 1. … 2. … 3. …

**One thing that took longer than I expected:** …

**One time block that went better than I expected (I'll protect it):** …

### Notes

- **Constraints are not leaks.** Work, caregiving, and commuting are fixed parts of your week, not wasted time. Build your plan around them.
- **Rough is fine.** Blocks of about 30 minutes are enough. A messy real log beats a tidy imagined one.
- **If tracking feels stressful,** keep it to a short line per day. You can skip anything that doesn't feel right.
- **Low-tech floor.** A sheet of paper with a line for each day and a few words per block.

**Options:** the Iowa State worksheet from Session 1, a paper grid, a spreadsheet, a notes app, or a short voice memo each evening.

??? example "Worked example"

    **Tracking method:** a paper grid on the fridge, filled in each evening.

    | Category | My prediction | My log | Gap (log minus prediction) |
    |----------|---------------|--------|----------------------------|
    | Classes or training | 12 | 12 | 0 |
    | Study | 10 | 6 | −4 |
    | Sleep | 49 | 44 | −5 |
    | Work or caregiving | 30 | 32 | +2 |
    | Screens (non-academic) | 14 | 25 | +11 |
    | Other | 53 | 49 | −4 |
    | **Total** | **168** | **168** | **0** |

    **Three time leaks:** 1. Phone checks between study blocks. 2. Switching to email in the middle of a task. 3. Waiting 20 to 30 minutes before starting each session.

    **Took longer than expected:** Wednesday's commute and errands. They ate the evening I had planned to study.

    **Went better than expected (protect it):** Saturday morning. I got 90 uninterrupted minutes of study.

## Handout 3-B: My Weekly System { #handout-3b }

*Session 2 · about 45 minutes*

A weekly system works in layers: fixed commitments first, then focus blocks for your hardest work, then a buffer. This handout holds one real upcoming week, a priority triage for it, and a one-line tool decision. Plan the week you actually have, not the one you wish you had.

**Download:** [PDF](../../../assets/intro%20to%20student%20success/handout-3-b-my-weekly-system.pdf)

### What goes on it

**Layer 1: Fixed commitments.** Classes, work, sleep, meals, caregiving, and anything else that can't move. …

**Layer 2: Focus blocks (3–5).** Give each block a specific task and a strategy from your [Strategy Toolkit card (Handout 2-D)](../module-2/materials.md#handout-2d).

| Day and time | Task | Strategy |
|--------------|------|----------|
|  |  |  |
|  |  |  |
|  |  |  |

**Layer 3: Buffer.** At least two blocks, and leave at least one block unscheduled. Buffer blocks: …

**Triage.** Write every task for the week on the grid, then circle the "important, not urgent" items. Make sure at least one is in a focus block.

| | Urgent | Not urgent |
|---|--------|------------|
| **Important** | **Do now:** … | **Schedule:** … |
| **Not important** | **Delegate or shorten:** … | **Drop or defer:** … |

**My tool decision (one line):** One planner or calendar: … One note system: … I chose them because: …

**My Sunday 15-minute reset:** day and time: …

### Notes

- **The buffer is not wasted time.** A plan with no slack breaks on the first surprise.
- **Use your Handout 1-A.** Put focus blocks where you focus best (see the condition you named in [Handout 1-A](../module-1/materials.md#handout-1a)).
- **Reset steps.** Once a week, look at the week ahead, move or cancel what won't fit, and name your top three tasks. It grows out of the weekly reflection prompt in [Handout 1-E](../module-1/materials.md#handout-1e).

**Options:** a digital calendar, a paper planner, a wall calendar, or sticky notes on a single sheet. Use whichever you will open every day.

??? example "Worked example"

    **Fixed commitments:** Work Monday to Friday, 8 to 4. Dinner at 6. Sleep 11:30 p.m. to 6:30 a.m. Sister's call Sunday afternoon.

    | Day and time | Task | Strategy |
    |--------------|------|----------|
    | Tue, 7 p.m. | Chapter 3 of the study guide | Blank-page retrieval, 10 minutes after reading |
    | Thu, 7 p.m. | Chapters 1–3 mixed practice questions | Day 3 retrieval, interleaved |
    | Sat, 9 a.m. | Chapter 4, one section at a time | SQ3R |

    **Buffer:** Wednesday evening and Friday evening stay free. Saturday afternoon is unscheduled.

    | | Urgent | Not urgent |
    |---|--------|------------|
    | **Important** | **Do now:** Submit the exam registration form. | **Schedule:** Study sessions (Tue, Thu, Sat). Ask the instructor about the practice exam. |
    | **Not important** | **Delegate or shorten:** Reply to group-chat questions in one batch. | **Drop or defer:** Reorganizing my notes app. |

    **Tool decision:** Paper planner for the calendar, phone notes for everything else. I'll open both every day.

    **Sunday reset:** 7 p.m. at the kitchen table.

## Handout 3-C: Break This Down { #handout-3c }

*Session 3 · about 25 minutes*

A big task feels less scary once it is steps with dates. This handout takes one real, scary task, breaks it into sequenced steps, and gives you an if–then plan for the step you're most likely to avoid. Pick something you are actually putting off, not a made-up task.

**Download:** [PDF](../../../assets/intro%20to%20student%20success/handout-3-c-break-this-down.pdf)

### What goes on it

**The task:** … **Deadline:** …

**Work backward.** Write at least 5 sequenced steps. Add a checkpoint (a date and a visible "done" signal) to at least two of them.

| Step | What I'll do | Checkpoint (date and "done" signal) |
|------|--------------|-------------------------------------|
| 1 |  |  |
| 2 |  |  |
| 3 |  |  |
| 4 |  |  |
| 5 |  |  |

**My next physical action** (the smallest visible thing that starts the task): …

**My if–then plan for the step I'm most likely to avoid:**

- **If** (a time or place cue) … **then** (a specific action) …
- **The feeling I expect when I start:** …
- **One thing I'll say to myself:** …

**Put it in my calendar:** yes / not yet

### Notes

- **Procrastination is mostly a mood problem.** You are not lazy. Name the feeling, shrink the first step, and use your [Week 1 reframe skills (Handout 1-B)](../module-1/materials.md#handout-1b).
- **If you start late, restart.** The next moment is a new chance to use the plan.
- **When it's more than procrastination.** If you're stuck on many tasks for weeks, or avoidance comes with persistent low mood or anxiety, talk with a counselor, doctor, or disability support office. [Module 4](../module-4/index.md) and the [Get Help](../../../get-help.md) page list places to start.

**Options:** written, a spreadsheet, or index cards in a row. One step per card makes it easy to reorder.

??? example "Worked example"

    **The task:** Write the final paper for my psychology class. **Deadline:** three weeks from today.

    | Step | What I'll do | Checkpoint |
    |------|--------------|------------|
    | 1 | Pick the topic and write a one-sentence question | End of Week 1, Monday. Question typed in the top of a blank doc. |
    | 2 | Find and read five sources, with notes in my own words | End of Week 1. Five sources in a list. |
    | 3 | Write an outline with one sentence per section | Start of Week 2. One-page outline. |
    | 4 | Write the first draft, one section a day | End of Week 2. Full draft, even if rough. |
    | 5 | Put the draft down for a day, then revise | Start of Week 3. Marked-up draft. |
    | 6 | Proofread and submit | Two days before the deadline. Submitted. |

    **My next physical action:** Open a blank document and type the title.

    **My if–then plan (for Step 4):** If it's 4 p.m. on a draft day, then I'll open the document and write one sentence.
    **The feeling I expect:** Anxious that it won't be good enough.
    **One thing I'll say to myself:** "It's a first draft. It only has to exist."

## Handout 3-D: Prep Plan { #handout-3d }

*Session 4 · about 35 minutes*

Cramming feels productive and leaves little behind. This handout works backward from a real deadline: three spaced sessions, at least one practice test, and an after-action review so the next plan is better. Use an exam, an assignment, a certification test, or a skill goal.

**Download:** [PDF](../../../assets/intro%20to%20student%20success/handout-3-d-prep-plan.pdf)

### What goes on it

**What I'm preparing for:** … **Date:** …

**What's being tested** (from the syllabus, past exams, or the instructor's hints): …

**Backward calendar.** Put the date at the bottom and work back. Use your [Handout 2-B spaced-practice schedule](../module-2/materials.md#handout-2b) if you've made one.

| When | Topic | Retrieval method (blank page, flashcards, practice questions) | My if–then start |
|------|-------|--------------------------------------------------------------|------------------|
| Session 1 |  |  |  |
| Session 2 |  |  |  |
| Session 3 |  |  |  |
| Practice test or draft review |  |  |  |
| Date of the exam or deadline |  |  |  |

**After-action review (answer afterward):**

1. What did I miss, and why? Was it knowledge, strategy, time, or stress?
2. What worked in my plan?
3. What will I change next time?

**Scheduled in my calendar:** yes / not yet

### Notes

- **Take at least one practice test** under realistic conditions (timed, closed-book).
- **Assignments work the same way.** Outline, draft, revise, with checkpoints and time to put the draft down for a day before revising.
- **No upcoming exam?** Use an assignment, a certification test, or a skill goal from your [SMART goals sheet (Handout 1-D)](../module-1/materials.md#handout-1d).
- **Deadline weeks away?** Use the Day 1 / 3 / 7 pattern first, then lengthen the gaps.

**Options:** a paper calendar, a spreadsheet, or index cards for each session.

??? example "Worked example"

    **What I'm preparing for:** My certification exam. **Date:** Friday, ten days from now.

    **What's being tested:** Chapters 1 to 4 (from the exam outline).

    | When | Topic | Retrieval method | My if–then start |
    |------|-------|------------------|------------------|
    | Day 1 (Monday) | Chapters 1–2 | Blank page, 10 minutes, then check | If it's Monday at 7 p.m., then I'll close the book and write what I remember. |
    | Day 3 (Wednesday) | Chapters 1–3, mixed | Practice questions from the study guide | If it's Wednesday at 7 p.m., then I'll do five questions before I check anything. |
    | Day 7 (Sunday) | Everything, starting with what I missed | Flashcards, then a blank page | If I feel too tired to start, then I'll do 10 minutes and decide whether to keep going. |
    | Practice test (Tuesday) | Full practice exam | Timed, closed-book | If it's Tuesday at 7 p.m., then I'll set the timer first. |
    | Exam (Friday) | | | |

    **After-action review:** What did I miss, and why? What worked? What will I change next time?

    **Scheduled in my calendar:** done.
