# Session 2 — The Weekly Operating System, Triage & Tools (≈70 min)

*Optional extras add about 5 minutes: a partner step.*

**Focus:** building a weekly plan, triaging a heavy week, and choosing tools. **CLOs:** 5.

**You'll Make:** weekly plan, triage note, and one-line tool decision ([Handout 3-B](materials.md#handout-3b)).

---

## Warm-up from memory (5 min)

Look at the days you've logged so far. Without checking [Handout 3-A](materials.md#handout-3a), name one pattern you've noticed (a time leak, or a block that went better than expected) and one thing you'll change or protect because of it. Then check your log.

---

## Core ideas (10 min)

- **Plan in layers.** Fixed commitments first (classes, work, sleep, meals, caregiving). Then focus blocks for your hardest work. Then a buffer. Whatever remains is flexible time. Don't start with the flexible time.
- **Protect focus time.** Choose blocks when you tend to focus best (the conditions on your [Handout 1-A](../module-1/materials.md#handout-1a) help here), and give each block a specific task rather than "study."
- **Use Week 2.** Put your toolkit strategies in the blocks: "Wed 4–5 p.m.: retrieval drill for Biology, Day 3." Your [Strategy Toolkit card](../module-2/materials.md#handout-2d) is the source.
- **The buffer is not wasted time.** Real weeks include late buses, slow tasks, and low-energy days. A plan with no slack breaks on the first surprise.
- **Triage with the Eisenhower Matrix.** Sort tasks by urgent and important. *Urgent and important:* do now. *Important, not urgent:* schedule it (this is where most of your learning lives). *Urgent, not important:* delegate, shorten, or batch. *Neither:* drop or defer.
- **Tools serve the system, not the reverse.** One planner or calendar and one note system is enough. Switching apps often is its own form of procrastination.
- **The Sunday 15-minute reset.** Once a week, look at the week ahead, move or cancel what won't fit, and name your top three tasks. It grows out of the weekly reflection prompt in [Handout 1-E](../module-1/materials.md#handout-1e).

---

## Learn (10 min)

- **Skim (5 min):** LSU Health Sciences Center, ["Time Management"](https://www.lsuhsc.edu/administration/academic/studentsuccess/time-management.aspx) student success page. Look at the weekly planner, prioritizing tasks, and tools sections.
- **Download (5 min):** the University of Central Florida's [fillable Eisenhower Matrix (PDF)](https://academicsuccess.ucf.edu/sarc/wp-content/uploads/sites/31/2020/12/Eisenhower-Matrix-Fillable.pdf). Or draw a 2×2 grid on paper.

---

## Do (45 min)

1. **Build your weekly plan (25 min).** Use one real upcoming week. Add fixed commitments first, then 3–5 focus blocks (each with a named task and a Week 2 strategy), then a buffer of at least two blocks. Leave at least one block unscheduled. Use [Handout 3-B](materials.md#handout-3b).
2. **Triage (10 min).** Write every task for the week on the Eisenhower Matrix. Circle the "important, not urgent" items and make sure at least one is in a focus block.
3. **Tool decision (5 min).** Choose one planner or calendar and one note system. Write your one-line decision on Handout 3-B.
4. **Schedule the reset (5 min).** Pick your Sunday reset time and put it in your calendar now.

**Optional partner step (5 min).** Trade plans and ask, "Where is your buffer?" and "What happens if this block gets cancelled?"

---

## Options

- **Digital calendar, paper planner, wall calendar, or sticky notes on a single sheet.** A tool is useful if you will open it daily, and a paper planner is a fully valid choice.
- **Focus-time methods.** Short timed work intervals (for example, 25 minutes on, 5 off) help some people start. Use them if they work for you, and drop them if they don't.
