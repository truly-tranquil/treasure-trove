## Core ideas (10 min)

- **Procrastination is mostly a mood problem, not a time problem.** Research suggests we often delay a task to get short-term relief from the feelings it triggers: boredom, anxiety, self-doubt, resentment. The relief is real, and so is the cost later.
- **It isn't laziness.** Calling yourself lazy usually makes it worse. It adds shame to the feeling you were already avoiding. Your [Week 1 reframe skills](../module-1/materials.md#handout-1b) apply here.
- **Make the first step tiny.** The "next physical action" is the smallest visible thing you can do: open the document, write one sentence, gather the materials. If you can't picture doing it, it's still too big.
- **If–then plans.** Pre-decide when and where you'll start: "If it's 4 p.m. on Tuesday, then I will open the document and write one sentence." Research on this kind of plan shows it helps people begin.
- **Work backward from the deadline.** Pick the due date, then set a checkpoint for each step working back to today. Big tasks feel less scary once they're steps with dates.
- **Other starters.** Lower the bar (two minutes), change the place, work next to someone, remove the most tempting distraction, or reward the start rather than the finish.
- **Competence, from Week 1.** One of the three needs in Self-Determination Theory is competence, the sense that you can grow (see [Handout 1-C](../module-1/materials.md#handout-1c)). If a step feels too hard to start, break it down until it feels doable.

## Do (25 min)

**Break this down.** Pick one scary, real assignment or task, and use [Handout 3-C](materials.md#handout-3c).

1. **Work backward (5 min).** Write the deadline, then work back to today.
2. **Chunk (10 min).** Write at least 5 sequenced steps. Add an interim checkpoint (a date and a visible "done" signal) to at least two of them.
3. **Name the next physical action (3 min).** What's the very first thing you'll do?
4. **Write one if–then plan (5 min)** for the step you're most likely to avoid. Add the feeling you expect to have when you start ("bored," "anxious") and one thing you'll say to yourself.
5. **Put it in your calendar (2 min).**

**Optional partner step (5 min).** Trade chunked tasks and ask, "Which step still feels too big?" Then split it.

## Options

- **Written, spreadsheet, or index cards in a row.** One step per card makes it easy to reorder.
- **Be kind.** If you start late, restart. The next moment is a new chance to use the plan.

!!! note "When it's more than procrastination"
    If you're stuck on many tasks for weeks, or avoidance comes with persistent low mood, anxiety, or trouble focusing, talk with a counselor, doctor, or disability support office. [Module 4](../module-4/index.md) covers how to find those supports, and the [Get Help](../../../get-help.md) page lists places to start.
