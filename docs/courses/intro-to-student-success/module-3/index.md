### End-of-week self-check · 15 min

Answer from memory first, then check your notes and the answers.

1. What are three common time leaks you found in your audit?
2. Name the four quadrants of the Eisenhower Matrix and what to do with each.
3. Why is procrastination often described as a mood problem rather than a time problem?
4. What is a "next physical action"?
5. What are the parts of an if–then plan?

??? note "Answers"

    1. Your own. Common examples are phone checks, task switching, and underestimated tasks.
    2. Do now (urgent and important), schedule (important, not urgent), delegate or shorten (urgent, not important), drop or defer (neither).
    3. People often avoid a task to get relief from the feelings it triggers, so working on the feeling and the first step helps more than finding more hours.
    4. The smallest visible action that starts a task.
    5. A time or place cue ("if…") and a specific action ("then…").

Then re-rate your confidence on the five items and compare with the start of the week.

## What you'll be able to do

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

1. Analyze where your time actually goes (a one-week time audit).
2. Apply a weekly planning routine that front-loads commitments and protects focus time.
3. Explain procrastination as largely an emotion-regulation problem and apply at least two strategies for getting started.
4. Break large tasks into sequenced, manageable steps with checkpoints, and prepare for an exam or assignment by working backward from the date.
5. Evaluate common productivity tools and choose one stack that fits your system (not the hype).

## What you'll have by the end

- [ ] A completed one-week time audit showing real vs. perceived time use, with at least three time leaks marked
- [ ] A working weekly plan (one real week, not a fantasy week) with fixed commitments, blocked focus time, and a buffer
- [ ] A short priority-triage note for a busy week (urgent vs. important vs. delegable or droppable)
- [ ] One large task broken into at least 5 sequenced steps with interim checkpoints, plus one "if–then" start plan for the step you're most likely to avoid
- [ ] An exam or assignment prep plan for one real upcoming deadline: worked backward from the date, with three spaced sessions, one practice test, and a post-exam or post-assignment review prompt
- [ ] A one-line tool decision (one planner or calendar plus one note system) with the reason it fits you

The weekly plan, if–then plan, and prep plan feed into your [Personal Student Success Plan](../capstone.md).
