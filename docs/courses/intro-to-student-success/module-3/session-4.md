# Session 4 — Exams & Assignments Without Cramming (≈85 min)

*Optional extras add about 5 minutes: a partner step.*

**Focus:** reviewing your time audit, and a backward-planned prep routine. **CLOs:** 5 (audit review), 7 (prep routine).

**You'll Make:** a completed time audit ([Handout 3-A](materials.md#handout-3a)) and an exam or assignment prep plan ([Handout 3-D](materials.md#handout-3d)).

---

## Warm-up from memory (5 min)

Write your if–then plan for the step you're most likely to avoid, from memory. Then check [Handout 3-C](materials.md#handout-3c).

---

## Core ideas (10 min)

- **Cramming feels productive and leaves little behind.** Last-minute study is mostly re-reading and holding facts in short-term memory. [Week 2's evidence](../module-2/session-1.md) points the other way: spacing and testing.
- **Work backward.** Start from the exam or deadline and place your sessions on the calendar first, so urgent days don't take them.
- **Three spaced sessions, at least one practice test.** Space the sessions across days, put retrieval inside each, and take at least one practice test under realistic conditions (timed, closed-book).
- **Know what's being tested.** Check the syllabus, past exams, and the instructor's hints. Turn the topics into a checklist.
- **Assignments work the same way.** Outline, draft, revise, with checkpoints and time to put the draft down for a day before revising.
- **After-action review.** After the exam or submission, ask, "What did I miss, and why?" Was it knowledge, strategy, time, or stress? Use the answer to adjust your plan for next time.

## Learn (15 min)

- **Read (15 min):** Cornell University Learning Strategies Center, ["Studying and Taking Exams"](https://lsc.cornell.edu/how-to-study/studying-for-and-taking-exams) and ["What To Do With Practice Exams."](https://lsc.cornell.edu/how-to-study/studying-for-and-taking-exams/what-to-do-with-practice-exams) Skim the first page, then read the practice-exams page more closely.

You'll reuse your Module 2 [spaced-practice schedule](../module-2/materials.md#handout-2b) and retrieval drill in the Do section below.

---

## Do (55 min)

### Finish your time audit (20 min)

By now you have close to a full week of data. On [Handout 3-A](materials.md#handout-3a):

1. Total your log for each category and fill in the "My log" column.
2. Work out the gap (log minus prediction) and note any surprises.
3. Mark at least three time leaks, one thing that took longer than you expected, and one time block that was better than you expected (protect this one).

Remember that work, caregiving, and commuting are constraints, not leaks. Then glance at your weekly plan from [Session 2](session-2.md). Does anything need to change?

### Write your prep plan (35 min)

Write a prep plan for one real deadline using [Handout 3-D](materials.md#handout-3d).

1. **Backward calendar (10 min).** Put the date at the bottom and work back. Place three spaced sessions and one practice test or draft review.
2. **Fill each session (15 min).** Name the topic, the retrieval method (blank page, flashcards, practice questions), and your if–then start.
3. **After-action review prompt (5 min).** Write the questions you'll answer afterward.
4. **Schedule it (5 min).** Put every session in your weekly plan and calendar.

**Optional partner step (5 min).** Trade plans and ask, "Which session is most likely to get bumped, and what's your backup time?"

## Options
